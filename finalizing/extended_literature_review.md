## E.1: Eliminating Credential Stuffing Attack Surfaces: A Comparative Analysis of OAuth 2.0 and Password-Based Authentication
**Authors:** Harsh Kumar, Isha Singh, Aakanksha Singh et al.
**Year:** 2026 | **Venue:** Various Academic Platforms | **Priority:** Important (2-star)
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6576013

**Methodology & Findings:**
Background: Credential stuffing attacks, which leverage breached credential databases to test username/password combinations against services, represent 24% of global security breaches. Traditional password-based authentication remains vulnerable despite defensive mechanisms like rate limiting and challenge-response systems.Objective: This study empirically evaluates OAuth 2.0 federated identity&amp;apos;s defensive effectiveness against credential stuffing attacks compared to traditional password-based authentication.Methods: A controlled testbed implemented parallel traditional authentication and OAuth 2.0 endpoints, then subjected both to identical credential stuffing attacks with 101 compromised credential pairs from real breach databases.Results: OAuth 2.0 achieved complete attack mitigation (0/101 successful breaches) versus traditional authentication&amp;apos;s 99.01% mitigation (1/101 breach). Attack attempts against OAuth were rejected as the credential submission surface does not exist in delegated architecture. OAuth unexpectedly exhibited 2.27% faster response times, contradicting delegation overhead assumptions.Conclusions: OAuth 2.0&amp;apos;s architectural delegation fundamentally eliminates credential stuffing attack surfaces rather than merely hardening against them, providing enterprise-grade security for resource-constrained organizations without requiring sophisticated threat intelligence infrastructure.

## E.2: Adaptive Anomaly Detection Algorithm for Real-Time Identification of Phishing and Credential Stuffing Attacks in E-Learning Systems Using Deep Reinforcement Learning
**Authors:** Nilufar Madaminova, Akram Maxammatov, Svetlana Sadchikova et al.
**Year:** 2026 | **Venue:** Journal of Internet Services and Information Security | **Priority:** Important (2-star)
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.58346/jisis.2026.i2.013

**Methodology & Findings:**
The quick embrace of e-learning software programs and cloud-based LMS has greatly enhanced
digital education through better access and scalability of the platform. Nevertheless, with the
reliance on the use of online authentication platforms, there is also a higher susceptibility to
cyberattacks such as phishing attacks and credential stuffing attacks. These attacks utilize
vulnerabilities in the user authentication process, stolen credentials, and deceptive communication
tactics to gain unauthorized access, steal academic credentials, and disrupt the entire learning
process. Traditional methods of intrusion detection and static machine learning have proven less
effective in adapting to dynamic attack patterns and have a high percentage of false positives. To
mitigate these problems, this paper proposes a novel Adaptive Anomaly Detection Algorithm that
uses Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to Real-Time Identify Phishing and Credential Stuffing
Attacks in E-Learning Systems. The proposed approach involves the use of behavioral analysis,
sequential anomaly detection, and adaptive policy optimization. Data pre-processing, behavioral feature extraction, state-action-based deep reinforcement learning, attack categorization, and
adaptive mechanisms will form the basis of the proposed algorithm. Among behavioral features
considered are failed login attempts, rate of IP change, session time, URL reputation, and device
mismatch. Evaluation through experiments was carried out using a mixed cybersecurity data set that
contained 125,000 instances with phishing attacks, credential stuffing, and legitimate users' actions.
The developed DRL model attained outstanding results with an accuracy of 98.4%, precision of
98.1%, recall of 97.8%, and F1-score of 97.9% while having a minimum of false positives of 1.9%.
A comparative study indicates remarkable improvements compared to SVM, Random Forest, CNN,
and LSTM techniques. The findings prove the effectiveness of adaptive reinforcement learning in
improving cybersecurity capabilities in contemporary e-learning environments.
