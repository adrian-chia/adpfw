CRYPTOGRAPHIC GROUND TRUTH ⏱ Target: 4:45 – 6:00

Ground Truth at the Wire: Anchoring in Cryptographic TLS Invariants

Why higher-layer HTTP headers lie, but transport-layer TLS handshakes tell the truth

Application Layer (L7) Is Trivial to Forge
Any script can modify the User-Agent to pretend it is Chrome 120 on macOS.
Headers like Accept-Language and Sec-CH-UA are trivially injected.
Relying on L7 headers alone is an invitation to be deceived.
Transport Layer (L4/TLS) Invariants
Client Hello Negotiation: Advertised cipher suites, extension sequences, elliptic curves, and compression methods.
High Cost of Forgery: Modifying cryptographic client parameters at scale introduces massive CPU overhead and broken handshakes for botnets.
Defeating "Cipher Stunting": Even when bots shuffle cipher suites to evade static JA3 hashes, multi-dimensional feature modeling captures the statistical anomaly.
Click to Zoom: Feature Distributions 🔍
[4:45 - 6:00] Highlight the technical depth of TLS fingerprinting.

Now, any cybersecurity researcher will tell you: machine learning is only as good as the features you feed it. If you train a model on User-Agent strings and HTTP headers, you have built a house on sand. Why? Because an attacker can spoof a User-Agent in literally one line of Python code.

That is why our methodology drops down to the transport layer — the TLS Client Hello handshake. When a genuine Chrome browser connects to your server, it advertises a very specific ordered list of cipher suites, supported elliptic curves, and TLS extensions.

Technical Nuance: "Automated bot tools like Python Requests or Go HTTP clients have distinct, hardcoded TLS signatures. Even advanced tools like curl_cffi that attempt browser impersonation exhibit subtle structural discrepancies or introduce cipher stunting — randomizing ciphers to evade static JA3 hash blacklists."

By pairing TLS invariants with behavioral telemetry, we create a feature space that is computationally expensive and operationally painful for an attacker to fake.

MODEL SELECTION & RATIONALE ⏱ Target: 7:15 – 8:30

Algorithmic Selection: Why TCNs Outshine LSTMs & Reinforcement Learning

Solving the latency, gradient stability, and training bottlenecks of sequence modeling

❌ Deep Reinforcement Learning
The Concept: Agent optimizes defensive throttling policy via reward functions.
The Fatal Flaw: Continuous reward exploration destabilizes operational routing policies. High convergence compute costs make it unviable inline.
Production Verdict: Too unstable for critical authentication paths.
⚠️ LSTMs / Recurrent Networks
The Concept: Models time series by passing hidden state vectors through recurrent gating cells.
The Fatal Flaw: Recurrent step-by-step dependency prevents parallel training. Suffers from vanishing gradient bottlenecks over extended historical horizons.
Production Verdict: High training latency and gradient degradation.
✅ Temporal ConvNets (TCNs)
The Concept: Dilated causal 1D convolutions with residual shortcut connections (Bai et al., 2018).
The Advantage: Fully parallelizable feedforward training, exponentially expanding receptive fields, and zero vanishing gradients.
Proven Efficacy: Outperforms LSTM/GRU baselines; achieved 96.72% on Edge-IIoTset intrusion benchmark.
Empirical Finding (Bai, Kolter & Koltun, 2018): Convolutional networks systematically outperform recurrent baselines across benchmark sequence problems while offering longer effective memory retention and order-of-magnitude faster training parallelization.
[7:15 - 8:30] Explain the algorithmic trade-offs clearly. Anticipate committee questions on LSTMs.

Now, let's address the most important modeling question: Why Temporal Convolutional Networks? When people hear 'time series sequence modeling', the default knee-jerk reaction in cybersecurity is often: 'Why didn't you use an LSTM or Deep Reinforcement Learning?'

Let's dismantle both options. Deep Reinforcement Learning sounds fancy, but in a live API Gateway, DRL exploration is catastrophic. If an agent tries an exploratory policy during a traffic shift, it might block thousands of legitimate paying users to test a hypothesis. We cannot afford that risk.

Recurrent models like LSTMs and GRUs have a different Achilles' heel: sequential execution. Because step 2 requires the hidden state of step 1, you cannot parallelize training across GPU or multi-core hardware. And over long sequences, LSTMs suffer from vanishing gradients.

The TCN Advantage: "TCNs use feedforward 1D causal convolutions. They evaluate all timesteps simultaneously during training. With residual shortcut connections and dilated filters, they capture longer historical memory than LSTMs while executing inference in a fraction of a millisecond."
DEEP LEARNING MECHANICS ⏱ Target: 8:30 – 9:45

Inside the TCN: Dilated Causal Convolutions & Receptive Field Geometry

Capturing 10-minute attack trajectories with only 28,241 parameters

Click to Zoom: TCN Architecture 🔍
1. Causal & Dilated Convolutions
Causal: Zero look-ahead bias; output at timestep $t$ depends strictly on $x_{0 \dots t}$.
Dilated: Dilation factors $d \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ expand the receptive field exponentially without pooling.
2. Receptive Field Geometry
Formula: $RF = 1 + 2 \times (k-1) \times \sum d_l = \mathbf{29\text{ steps}}$.
Because $RF = 29 > L = 10$, the model possesses 100% contextual visibility across the entire 10-minute sequence without edge truncation.
3. Compact Parameter Budget
Total Trainable Weights: 28,241 parameters.
Channel capacity: 4 → 16 → 32 → 64.
Ultra-lightweight footprint allows instant in-memory loading and sub-millisecond CPU inference.
[8:30 - 9:45] Walk through the convolutional block math and receptive field.

Let's look under the hood of the neural network. How does this architecture achieve both deep temporal vision and sub-millisecond execution?

The network consists of three stacked residual blocks. The core engine uses two mathematical properties:

First, Causal Padding: A causal filter ensures that the prediction for minute 10 cannot peek into minute 11. It only convolves over past and present data, completely eliminating look-ahead bias.

Second, Dilations: Instead of convolving over adjacent entries, each subsequent layer skips inputs by a factor of $d = 1, 2, 4$. This increases the receptive field exponentially.

The Math: "Our formula yields a total receptive field of 29 time steps. Since our input window is L=10 minutes, 29 is nearly three times larger than the sequence length. That means when the network makes a decision at minute 10, it has unobstructed, full-fidelity visibility across the entire 10-minute attack trajectory."

And best of all: the entire network has only 28,241 parameters! It is smaller than a JPEG thumbnail.

Click to Zoom: Training Convergence 🔍
[11:00 - 12:15] Emphasize methodological hygiene — no data leakage!

Now, how did we turn 8 million raw database logs into training data? We aggregated raw request points into 3,187 contiguous 1-minute temporal windows.

To establish ground truth, we applied a strict dual-condition labeling heuristic: a window is marked as an attack if both the failure rate exceeds 30% AND the unique IP count exceeds 5. Why both? Because if an individual human mistypes their password three times, their failure rate is high, but their unique IP count is 1. That is NOT an attack. Requiring both conditions prevents false alarms on human typos.

This produced 1,595 attack windows and 1,592 benign windows — an exceptionally balanced dataset.

Methodological Rigor: "In machine learning research, the most common sin is random shuffling of time-series data. If you shuffle, future information leaks into the past. We enforced a strict 70/15/15 chronological split. Our StandardScaler normalization was fitted strictly on the first 70% of the timeline."

As you can see in the loss curve, training converged smoothly over 30 epochs, reaching a validation loss approaching zero at epoch 27.
